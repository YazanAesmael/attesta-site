The best transcription apps for iPhone (2026)

Guides · Updated July 2026

The right transcription app depends on what you are recording and what you value: accuracy, price, privacy, or a structured summary instead of a wall of text. It also depends on where the conversation happens — a video call and an in-person conversation around a table are not the same audio problem, and not every app handles both. Here are the strongest options on iPhone in 2026, what each is genuinely good at, and how to pick.

What actually matters when choosing

  • Accuracy is mostly your audio quality. Any good app handles clean speech; all of them struggle with crosstalk and heavy accents.
  • Transcript vs insights. Some apps give you raw text. Others pull out a summary, action items, and decisions, which is the real time-saver.
  • Where the conversation happens. Apps built around Zoom/Teams calls get a clean, separate audio feed per person for free. A single phone recording an in-person conversation has to separate voices from one microphone — a different, harder problem.
  • Privacy. Check whether the tool trains AI on your audio by default. It matters for interviews, client calls, and anything sensitive.
  • Pricing model. Per-seat subscription vs pay-per-minute. If you record occasionally, paying per minute is usually cheaper.
  • Reliability and billing. The complaints that actually drive people to switch apps are rarely about accuracy — they're stuck "processing" screens, recordings that vanish, and free trials that quietly turn into a charge. Worth checking specifically before you commit (more below).

What to watch out for, beyond accuracy

Across this category, the reviews that actually move people to switch apps are rarely about transcription quality. They are about trusting the tool with something you cannot record twice — a lecture, a doctor's visit, a one-time interview. Four things worth checking before you commit to any transcription app:

  • Can you see the price before you're charged it? A free trial that quietly converts to an annual charge, or a cost that's unclear until after you've committed, is the single most common complaint in this category.
  • What happens if processing gets stuck? Some apps leave a recording "analyzing" indefinitely with no error and no retry — you can't tell if it's working or gone.
  • Is the recording safe before it's transcribed? If the audio only exists in one place until the AI step runs, a failure there can cost you something you can't record again.
  • Is cancelling actually one tap? If it routes through a support ticket or a buried settings page instead of your phone's own subscription management, that's a sign the trial was built to be sticky, not simple.

Otter — best for online team meetings

The well-known name, strong on Zoom and Teams with live transcription and meeting notes. Per-seat subscription with a capped free tier, and its terms have allowed training on user data by default, so check the current version if privacy matters.

Rev — best for human-grade accuracy

Offers both AI and human transcription. When you need the cleanest possible transcript of a critical recording and will pay for it, Rev's human option is the accuracy benchmark.

Notta — best for multilingual

Broad language support across web and mobile, a good pick if you work across languages and want transcripts more than a structured summary.

Granola — best for polished, desktop-first meeting notes

Built around live video calls rather than in-person recording, with clean, minimal meeting notes that consistently draw strong reviews. A good pick if most of your conversations already happen on a screen and you want a Mac-native notes layer on top — less suited to a conversation happening in the room with you.

Voicenotes — best for asking your own notes

Builds a personal library of voice notes you can query later — ask it what you said about a topic instead of scrolling back through old recordings. Worth a look if you want to search and reason across everything you have recorded, not just read a single transcript.

Apple Voice Memos — best free and built-in

Free, on-device, already on your iPhone. It gives you audio and raw text, but no structured summary, speaker labels, or cross-device insights. Fine for a quick note, thin for a record you need to act on.

In-person vs. online: not every app handles both

Most transcription apps are built and tuned around the video-call case: a Zoom, Teams, or Meet window where every participant already has their own microphone. That is a much easier audio problem than a single iPhone on a conference table picking up two, three, or four people at once — and it shows. Speaker separation in particular tends to be the first thing that breaks down once a tool moves from "one clean feed per person" to "one shared microphone in a room."

If most of what you record is online meetings, a call-first tool like Otter or Granola will serve you well. If what you need transcribed is a conversation actually happening in front of you — an interview, a client meeting, a lecture, a doctor's visit — look for an app built around that case specifically. Frequently asked questions

What is the most accurate transcription app?

Accuracy depends on audio quality more than brand. Rev's human transcription is the benchmark; among AI apps, the cloud services are all strong on clean speech, with errors clustering around names, jargon, and crosstalk.

Does Apple have a transcription app?

Yes, Apple Voice Memos transcribes on-device for free, and Notes and Phone have built-in transcription. They give raw text, not a structured summary with action items.

Is there a free app that transcribes audio?

Apple Voice Memos (free, on-device), Otter's capped free tier, or Attesta's free trial plus pay-per-minute credits that never expire.

Can ChatGPT transcribe audio?

Not from a recording on its own, it needs a speech-to-text step first and does not do speaker labels or timestamps. A transcription app does all of that in one pass.

Does a transcription app work for in-person conversations, not just video calls?

Not always as well as it works on a call. Many transcription apps are built around an online meeting, where every participant already has a clean, separate microphone feed. A single phone on a table picking up several people at once is a harder audio problem, and speaker separation is usually the first thing that breaks down. Look for an app that names in-person, multi-speaker recording as a specific case rather than assuming a video-call layout.

Which transcription app doesn't train on my data?

Check each app's current terms yourself rather than trusting a marketing claim, since policies change. Attesta never trains a model on your audio or transcript — it charges per minute for the transcription and insights themselves, so there is no separate incentive to do anything else with your recordings.

What happens if a transcription job gets stuck processing?

Some apps leave a recording "processing" or "analyzing" indefinitely, with no way to tell if it's still working or has silently failed. Attesta's jobs either complete or land in an explicit failed state — a background check catches any job that stalls and marks it failed, so you always know where a recording stands.

What should I check before starting a free trial with a transcription app?

Look for the price you'll actually be charged, and when, before you start the trial rather than after. A trial that silently converts to an annual charge, or a price that's unclear until you've already committed, is the most common billing complaint in this category.
