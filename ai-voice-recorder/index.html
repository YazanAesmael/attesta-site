An AI voice recorder — no extra hardware

Guides · Updated July 2026

Most “AI voice recorders” are gadgets — a pendant or a clip you buy, charge, and carry everywhere. Attesta turns the iPhone already in your pocket into one. Tap record, and you get a speaker-labeled transcript with a summary, action items, and decisions. No device to buy, an audible consent tone so the room knows, and your audio is never used to train AI.

What an AI voice recorder does

A plain voice recorder gives you an audio file. An AI voice recorder goes further: it transcribes what was said and pulls out the meaning — a short summary, the key topics, who said what, the action items, and the decisions. Instead of scrubbing through audio later, you read a searchable record and jump straight to the part you need.

No hardware required

Wearable recorders like Plaud and similar pendants work, but they come with trade-offs: another few hundred dollars, another battery to charge, another thing to forget at home, and often a subscription on top of the hardware. Attesta needs none of that. Your iPhone already has a good microphone and is always with you — Attesta just adds the recording, transcription, and AI summary on top.

Why speaker labels matter

Most recordings worth transcribing involve more than one voice — a call, an interview, a quick conversation you want to remember accurately. A flat transcript of that turns into a wall of text you cannot follow. Compare a raw transcript of a two-person call…

so are we still on for Thursday yes but can we push it an hour sure that works I will send the new invite

…with the same audio, speaker-labeled:

Maria: So are we still on for Thursday?
James: Yes, but can we push it an hour?
Maria: Sure, that works. I will send the new invite.

Attesta separates the distinct voices in a recording automatically and labels them Speaker 1, Speaker 2, and so on — no setup, no separate mic per person. That labeling is also what lets the action items and decisions in your summary come out attributed, instead of a block of text you have to re-listen to just to work out who said what.

What you get from every recording

  • A speaker-labeled transcript — see who said what.
  • A summary of the conversation in a few lines.
  • Action items and decisions, pulled out automatically.
  • Key topics, quotes, and people mentioned for fast recall.
  • Pause and resume mid-recording — an interruption does not force you to start over.
  • Sync across every device you sign into, so a recording from your phone is there the next time you check.

Private by design

Recording other people is a matter of trust. Attesta is built around that: every recording opens with an audible tone so everyone present knows, your audio and transcript are yours to keep or delete, and nothing is ever used to train an AI model. We are paid to transcribe your minutes, not to mine your conversations.

Hardware recorders like Plaud are popular but add cost and another device. For an AI voice recorder without the gadget, Attesta records on your iPhone and returns a structured summary with action items and decisions.

Is there a free AI voice recorder?

Attesta has a free trial and pay-per-minute credits that never expire, so there is no subscription to start. Apple Voice Memos is free but gives you raw audio and text, not AI insights.

Does an AI voice recorder transcribe automatically?

Yes — after you stop, Attesta transcribes the audio and generates a speaker-labeled transcript plus a summary, key topics, action items, and people mentioned.

Can an AI voice recorder tell different speakers apart?

Yes — Attesta labels distinct voices as Speaker 1, Speaker 2, and so on, so a conversation between two or more people reads as a conversation instead of one unbroken block of text.

How long can one recording be?

A single recording auto-stops at 60 minutes. Pause and resume stay within that same recording, so a call that gets interrupted or a short break does not cost you your place or start a new file.
